**Version:** 0.1

# Executive Summary

Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) is often misunderstood as a collection of digital platforms.

Digital identity.

Digital payments.

Data exchange.

Government portals.

Cloud infrastructure.

These technologies are important.

But Digital Public Infrastructure is fundamentally **an institutional coordination system**, not a technology programme.

Within the Institutional Operating System (IOS), DPI provides the digital layer through which institutions become interoperable, services become integrated, and governance becomes scalable.

It enables governments to move from fragmented service delivery toward coordinated public capability.

---

# Definition

**Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)** is the foundational set of interoperable digital systems, standards, institutions, and governance arrangements that enable governments, markets, and citizens to interact securely, efficiently, and at scale.

DPI is public infrastructure.

It is not merely digital infrastructure.

# Why Digital Public Infrastructure Matters

Modern governments increasingly operate across multiple institutions.

Without interoperability:

* data remain fragmented
* services duplicate effort
* citizens repeat the same processes
* procurement becomes disconnected
* institutions lose efficiency

Digital Public Infrastructure enables institutions to function as one integrated system.

---

# Core Components of Digital Public Infrastructure

## 1. Digital Identity

Identity enables trusted participation.

Functions include:

* citizen authentication
* business verification
* service access
* secure transactions

Identity creates institutional trust.

---

## 2. Digital Payments

Public payment infrastructure enables:

* government transfers
* tax collection
* procurement payments
* social protection
* financial inclusion

Payment infrastructure supports economic coordination.

---

## 3. Data Exchange

Institutions require trusted information sharing.

Data exchange enables:

* interoperability
* evidence-based decisions
* coordinated service delivery
* reduced duplication

Data become institutional infrastructure.

---

## 4. Digital Registries

Registries provide authoritative public records.

Examples include:

* population registers
* business registers
* land records
* procurement registries
* supplier databases

Registries reduce institutional uncertainty.

---

## 5. Interoperability Standards

Technology alone cannot integrate institutions.

Shared standards enable:

* system compatibility
* secure communication
* reusable infrastructure
* coordinated governance

Standards make scale possible.

---

## 6. Governance Layer

Technology requires governance.

DPI governance includes:

* legal frameworks
* institutional authority
* cybersecurity
* privacy
* accountability
* oversight

Without governance, infrastructure becomes fragmented.

## Reusability

---

## Security

---

## Privacy

Digital capability should strengthen individual rights rather than weaken them.

---

## Institutional Sustainability

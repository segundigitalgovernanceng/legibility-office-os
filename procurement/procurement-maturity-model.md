# Legibility Office Procurement Maturity Model (PMM)

## Version

1.0

---

# Purpose

The Procurement Maturity Model (PMM) provides a structured framework for assessing the evolution of procurement capability within governments, public institutions, and development organizations.

Rather than measuring compliance alone, the model evaluates procurement as an institutional capability that creates public value, strengthens governance, and supports long-term national competitiveness.

---

# Core Principle

Procurement maturity is measured by the institution's ability to consistently transform public resources into sustainable institutional capability.

---

# Maturity Levels

## Level 1

### Administrative Procurement

Primary Objective

Purchase goods and services.

Characteristics

- Paper-based processes
- Compliance-driven
- Fragmented procurement
- Limited planning
- Weak supplier management
- Minimal performance measurement

Success Metric

Transactions completed.

---

## Level 2

### Operational Procurement

Primary Objective

Improve efficiency.

Characteristics

- Electronic procurement
- Standard operating procedures
- Basic procurement planning
- Supplier databases
- Contract management
- Performance reporting

Success Metric

Lower cost and faster procurement.

---

## Level 3

### Strategic Procurement

Primary Objective

Support institutional priorities.

Characteristics

- Category management
- Strategic sourcing
- Market intelligence
- Risk assessment
- Supplier performance measurement
- Procurement analytics

Success Metric

Procurement contributes to organizational strategy.

---

## Level 4

### Institutional Procurement

Primary Objective

Strengthen institutional capability.

Characteristics

- Procurement integrated with governance
- Institutional memory
- Cross-government coordination
- Digital Public Infrastructure integration
- Knowledge management
- Capability measurement

Success Metric

Procurement strengthens institutional resilience.

---

## Level 5

### Capability-Driven Procurement

Primary Objective

Create national capability.

Characteristics

- Industrial policy alignment
- Local supplier development
- Innovation procurement
- Public value optimization
- AI-assisted procurement intelligence
- Institutional learning loops

Success Metric

Procurement improves national competitiveness.

# Assessment Questions

## Governance

- Is procurement governed by clear legislation?
- Are procurement decisions auditable?
- Is accountability clearly assigned?

---

## Planning

- Are procurement plans linked to strategy?
- Is demand forecasting systematic?
- Is market intelligence used?

---

## Execution

- Are suppliers evaluated consistently?
- Are contracts actively monitored?
- Are delivery outcomes measured?

---

## Digital

- Is procurement digital?
- Are procurement systems interoperable?
- Is Digital Public Infrastructure integrated?

---

## Learning

- Are procurement lessons preserved?
- Is institutional memory maintained?
- Are procurement decisions evidence-based?

---

## Capability

- Does procurement strengthen institutions?
- Does procurement build domestic capability?
- Does procurement improve long-term resilience?

# Institutional Physics v1.0

## Toward a Science of Institutional Behaviour

**Version:** 1.0

**Project:** Legibility Office Institutional Operating System (IOS)

---

# Executive Summary

Physics explains how physical systems behave through universal principles.

Institutional Physics proposes that public institutions also exhibit observable, repeatable, and analyzable behaviour governed by structural laws rather than isolated events.

Governments do not fail or succeed randomly.

They respond to forces.

They accumulate inertia.

They experience friction.

They generate momentum.

They reach tipping points.

They consume energy.

They follow path-dependent trajectories.

Institutional Physics provides a systems language for understanding why institutions behave the way they do.

---

# Purpose

Institutional Physics seeks to answer:

**Can institutional behaviour be understood using principles analogous to physical systems?**

Rather than treating governance failures as isolated events, Institutional Physics studies recurring structural dynamics that shape institutional performance over time.

---

# Core Principle

> Institutions behave as dynamic systems.

> Their performance is governed less by individual decisions than by the architecture through which those decisions flow.

---

# Fundamental Assumptions

Institutional systems:

* accumulate history
* resist sudden change
* experience friction
* require energy
* respond to incentives
* adapt through feedback
* exhibit nonlinear behaviour
* produce emergent outcomes

# The Institutional State

Every institution exists in a measurable state defined by:

* capability
* trust
* legitimacy
* coordination
* knowledge
* resources
* execution quality

Institutional behaviour reflects interactions among these variables.

---

# The First Law of Institutional Physics

## Institutional Inertia

> Institutions tend to preserve their existing operating patterns unless acted upon by sufficiently strong internal or external forces.

Examples:

* legacy procurement systems
* outdated regulations
* administrative routines
* inherited bureaucratic structures

Reform requires force.

---

# The Second Law

## Institutional Force

Institutional change results from interacting forces.

Examples include:

* political leadership
* legislation
* fiscal incentives
* technology
* crisis
* public demand
* international pressure

The magnitude of change depends on both force and institutional inertia.

---

# The Third Law

## Institutional Friction

Every reform encounters resistance.

Sources include:

* bureaucracy
* conflicting incentives
* information asymmetry
* organizational complexity
* legal constraints
* institutional culture

Friction increases implementation cost.

---

# The Fourth Law

## Institutional Momentum

Successful reforms generate momentum.

Momentum increases when:

* institutions learn
* trust improves
* coordination strengthens
* capability compounds

Momentum reduces future reform costs.

---

# The Fifth Law

## Institutional Energy

Institutions require continuous energy.

Sources include:

* political commitment
* financial resources
* skilled personnel
* technological capability
* public legitimacy

Without energy, institutions decay.

---

# The Sixth Law

## Institutional Gravity

Large institutions attract additional responsibilities, resources, authority, and complexity.

Without corresponding capability growth, gravity eventually produces overload.

---

# The Seventh Law

## Institutional Equilibrium

Institutions naturally seek stable operating states.

Equilibrium may be:

* productive
* stagnant
* inefficient
* resilient

Reform shifts equilibrium.

---

# The Eighth Law

## Institutional Entropy

Without continuous learning and maintenance, institutional capability deteriorates.

Symptoms include:

* fragmented coordination
* outdated systems
* declining trust
* rising inefficiency

Entropy is inevitable unless actively managed.

---

# The Ninth Law

## Institutional Feedback

Institutions improve through feedback.

```text id="feedback-loop"
Decision

↓

Execution

↓

Evidence

↓

Institutional Memory

↓

Learning

↓

Improved Decision
```

Feedback converts experience into capability.

---

# The Tenth Law

## Institutional Path Dependence

Current institutional behaviour is constrained by historical decisions.

Examples:

* legal traditions
* procurement models
* administrative structures
* technological choices

History influences future capability.

# Institutional Mass

Institutional mass represents accumulated complexity.

* regulations
* agencies
* procedures
* reporting requirements

Mass alone does not increase capability.

* interoperability

* effective governance

Velocity without governance increases risk.

# Institutional Acceleration

* leadership

# Institutional Pressure

Pressure emerges when institutional demand exceeds capability.

* population growth
* fiscal stress
* technological disruption
* climate shocks

# Institutional Resilience

Resilience measures the ability to recover after disruption.

Resilient institutions:

* continue operating

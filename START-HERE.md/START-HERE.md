# Legibility Office Institutional Operating System

**An open-source reference implementation for designing, understanding, and improving institutional capability.**

> Policy creates intent.
> Governance creates authority.
> Procurement allocates capability.
> Institutions retain knowledge.
> Execution creates outcomes.
> Public value creates legitimacy.

The **Institutional Operating System (IOS)** brings these capabilities together into one open architecture.

---

## What Is the Institutional Operating System?

Governments have policies.

They have ministries, agencies, regulators, procurement systems, digital infrastructure, budgets, programmes, data, and increasingly AI systems.

But these components are often designed and operated separately.

The result is institutional fragmentation.

The IOS provides a common architecture for understanding how these components connect and how institutional capability can compound over time.

It is designed to help governments, researchers, developers, development organisations, and institutional practitioners answer one fundamental question:

**Can an institution repeatedly turn policy intent into public value?**

---

# The Core Model

The IOS is built around the **Capability Stack Framework**.

```text
                    PUBLIC VALUE
                         ▲
                         │
                EXECUTION CAPACITY
                         ▲
                         │
              INSTITUTIONAL CAPABILITY
                         ▲
                         │
                PROCUREMENT SYSTEMS
                         ▲
                         │
               GOVERNANCE ARCHITECTURE
                         ▲
                         │
                  POLICY INTENT
```

These layers are not simply project stages.

They are reinforcing institutional capabilities.

When capability compounds across the stack, institutions become more resilient, adaptive, interoperable, and capable of sustained delivery.

---

# Why This Exists

Traditional development models often follow:

```text
Finance → Project → Asset
```

The IOS examines a deeper institutional chain:

```text
Policy
   ↓
Governance
   ↓
Procurement
   ↓
Capability
   ↓
Execution
   ↓
Public Value
```

The distinction matters.

A project can be completed without creating durable institutional capability.

An institution with strong capability can repeatedly deliver projects, learn from them, retain knowledge, and improve future outcomes.

The objective is therefore not simply:

**Build the project.**

It is:

**Build the institution that can keep building.**

# The Capability Stack

The central diagnostic framework is:

### 1. Policy Intent

What should the country achieve?

### 2. Governance Architecture

Who has authority to decide, coordinate, regulate, and execute?

### 3. Procurement Systems

How are resources, technology, suppliers, standards, and knowledge allocated?

### 4. Institutional Capability

Can institutions retain expertise, knowledge, processes, infrastructure, and organisational memory?

### 5. Execution Capacity

Can institutions consistently transform capability into delivery?

### 6. Public Value

Does institutional activity produce stronger services, markets, resilience, productivity, trust, inclusion, and prosperity?

---

# The Institutional Physics

IOS treats institutions as systems rather than collections of departments.

Institutional outcomes emerge from interactions between:

```text
Authority
+
Capability
+
Information
+
Resources
+
Coordination
+
Execution
+
Learning
```

Weakness in one component can constrain the entire system.

The purpose of institutional analysis is therefore to identify the constraint and determine whether the system can adapt.

# Procurement

Procurement is treated as strategic infrastructure.

Every major public system enters the institution through some combination of:

```text
Procurement
   ↓
Supplier
   ↓
Technology
   ↓
Knowledge
   ↓
Capability
   ↓
Institutional Memory
```

The question is therefore not simply:

**Did government buy the system?**

It is:

**What capability did government acquire, retain, and compound through the procurement?**

---

# Institutional Memory

Institutions become stronger when learning survives individual people and political cycles.

IOS therefore treats:

* decisions
* standards
* lessons learned
* datasets
* procedures
* institutional knowledge
* implementation experience

as reusable institutional assets.

The objective is to reduce:

> institutional amnesia.

# The Goal

The goal is not to create another policy framework.

The goal is to create an **open institutional reference architecture** that can be studied, implemented, tested, improved, and reused.

The long-term ambition is simple:

> **Make institutional capability legible, measurable, reusable, and capable of compounding.**

If institutions can learn faster, coordinate better, retain knowledge longer, procure more strategically, execute more consistently, and continuously create public value, national competitiveness becomes an institutional capability rather than a political aspiration.
